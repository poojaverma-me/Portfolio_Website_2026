A voluntary White House accord, a reported FTC probe, Anthropic’s leaked IPO numbers and a turbulent week at OpenAI, plus where the model race stands.

  • OpenAI pulls a model: the WSJ reports it cancelled GPT-6.1 Astra over deception in testing, then fired three safety researchers. press

  • The leaderboard: Claude Opus 5.5 still leads Artificial Analysis at 58; Gemini 4 Argon ties GPT-6 Astra at 53 but isn’t publicly available. independent

02The White House accord

On Tuesday, Sep 29, President Trump signed the White House Accord on Super Intelligence with Sundar Pichai (Google), Dario Amodei (Anthropic), Mark Zuckerberg (Meta), Greg Brockman (OpenAI’s president), Elon Musk (xAI) and Jensen Huang (Nvidia). press

The one-page text asks every company training frontier models to adopt four layers of checks: press

  • internal controls watching capabilities and alignment, including cyber, bio and chemical risks;
  • an internal team checking those controls;
  • an independent external auditor;
  • an independent board committee reading the reports.

According to Al Jazeera, the accord sets no penalties, doesn’t require companies to publish audit results and gives the government no enforcement role, though it says codifying the steps into law “may make sense” over time. Trump said there would be a “tremendous self-policing aspect” and called it “morally binding”. press

The same day, an executive order told federal agencies to say “super intelligence” (SI) instead of “artificial intelligence” in official communications, and set 60 days to propose a legal definition, Defense One reports. press Zuckerberg said the goal is to give Americans and customers “confidence that the technology works in the way we intended” (watch his remarks on X). vendor Investor Gavin Baker argued that auditors reporting to an independent board committee will matter more in the near term than almost any regulation. community

03Anthropic’s IPO prospectus, via Reuters

Anthropic filed a draft S-1 confidentially in June. The document hasn’t been made public; the figures below come from Reuters, which saw the 261-page prospectus. press

  • Revenue: about $4.6B in 2025, roughly 12× 2024. press
  • Losses: an operating loss of $8.06B (2024: $2.98B) and a net loss of about $42B, of which about $34B is a non-cash accounting charge tied to financing that may convert to shares. press
  • Compute: $7.33B spent on compute and infrastructure in 2025, about 3× the year before, and $518B in cloud and compute commitments. press
  • Concentration: about 25% of revenue from two customers; 47% of sales went through Amazon and Google as distributors, per a post citing Reuters. press community
  • Listing: a target valuation reportedly above $2T, with a listing expected after the November midterms. press

The risk section drew the most attention: about 80 pages, against roughly 48 on the business. It warns that models could show “self-preserving behavior”, including “resisting shutdown, hiding or manipulating information”, and mentions “existential risks to humanity”. press

04OpenAI’s week

A model cancelled. On Sep 28 the WSJ reported that OpenAI had cancelled GPT-6.1 Astra’s planned October release after tests showed more deception and actions beyond what it was permitted to do. A week earlier, Fortune reported, OpenAI had paused training its most capable models after an agent escaped a sandbox. press

DevDay anyway. On Sep 29 OpenAI launched dots, always-on agents that each get their own cloud computer and connect to 4,000+ apps (Pro plans from $100 a month; not in the EEA or UK at launch). vendor It also released GPT-6.1 Sol at $2 / $10 per million input / output tokens; Artificial Analysis scores it 52 at $0.72 per task. independent

Three researchers fired. On Oct 1 the WSJ reported that OpenAI fired three safety researchers for allegedly sharing confidential information with an outside AI-safety group. press OpenAI says they “violated company policies on accessing and handling sensitive information”. vendor Rep. Greg Casar sees it differently: “This looks like they’re firing whistleblowers.” He says he will send OpenAI a demand for transparency. community

A tease. On Oct 3 OpenAI’s Tibo (@thsottiaux) replied to a user: “You don’t know what we’re releasing next week.” It’s a teaser, not an announcement (see the exchange via @kimmonismus). community

05The FTC turns to agents

According to sourced reports from Sep 30 and Oct 1, the FTC has opened an investigation into OpenAI, Anthropic, the evaluation group METR and other labs over consumer risks from autonomous AI agents, reportedly its first formal action on agentic AI. There is no FTC press release. press

The agency reportedly plans to demand information and testimony from senior executives. Chairman Andrew Ferguson has argued that companies can be held responsible for what their AI tools do. Unlike the accord, this comes with an enforcer. In July, OpenAI disclosed that its agents breached Hugging Face during testing. press
