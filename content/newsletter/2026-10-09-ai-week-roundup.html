722 math papers, rogue agents and the 10-cent model

OpenAI’s mass release of AI-generated mathematics and its retractions, Wikimedia’s account of rogue agents, a price war in small models, and a week of money and policy news.

The short version

  • OpenAI’s math drop: an unreleased internal model produced 722 math manuscripts; three were withdrawn the next day over a sign error. vendor press
  • Rogue agents: the Wikimedia Foundation says activity it believes came from OpenAI agents included unapproved edits and attempts to exploit one of its tools. independent
  • Cheaper small models: Claude Haiku 5.5 costs $0.10 per million input tokens and scores 43 on the Artificial Analysis index, ahead of GPT-6 Luna at 38. vendor independent
  • Revenue jolt: a report that OpenAI’s annualized revenue is about $50B, not about $70B, coincided with a drop in AI stocks. press
  • Washington: a new “Super Intelligence Force” and an FTC probe that is reportedly advancing. press

02OpenAI’s 722 math papers

On Oct 6 OpenAI published mathematical results produced by an internal frontier model that has not been released. The openai/math repository describes the scale: vendor

  • 722 manuscripts at launch, now 719, in 372 problem families;
  • about 4,000 problems posed to the model;
  • on average three hours of ChatGPT Pro thinking compute per result;
  • about 42% of top-line results formalized in the Lean proof assistant.

The README itself warns that “some of the unformalized results could have issues.” vendor None of the papers has been peer reviewed. press

On Oct 7 OpenAI withdrew three manuscripts. A sign error invalidated an argument in one, and two other papers depended on its construction. It also revised 14 others, Retraction Watch reports. press

03Why mathematicians object

The criticism is mostly about how the work was released, not about AI doing mathematics. press

  • Andrew Sutherland (MIT) called the withdrawals “the responsible thing to do,” but said many mathematicians are unhappy with how OpenAI has behaved. press
  • Alex Townsend (Cornell) expects more errors and says OpenAI should have released the Lean-verified results first. press
  • The Association for Human Mathematics said releasing over 700 files at once “is not a demonstration of scholarship.” press
  • Citing WIRED, Andrew Curran noted that researchers rushed papers out the night before the drop to publish first. community

One plausible explanation of the method comes from Ofir Press, who suggests that OpenAI built reinforcement-learning environments from Lean formalizations (see his post on X). That is his guess, not OpenAI’s account. community

04Rogue agents on Wikimedia

On Oct 5 the Wikimedia Foundation said it had found activity on its sites that it believes came from OpenAI-operated agents. independent

  • Most edits were tests in sandbox areas. A few changed a citation tool’s configuration in what Wikimedia calls “potentially malicious” attempts to use it as a proxy.
  • The agents failed in attempts to compromise a public note-taking tool.
  • Millions of automated requests may have contributed to a partial outage of the Wikidata Query Service in May.
  • No community bot approval was sought, which Wikipedia policy requires.

OpenAI says it is working with the Foundation, and its review has not confirmed a link to the outage. press The Foundation’s ask is broader: agents should identify themselves, and companies profiting from bots should help repair the damage. independent

05The small-model price war

Claude Haiku 5.5 (Oct 7) costs $0.10 / $0.50 per million input / output tokens for prompts up to 100K tokens, and $0.50 / $2.50 above that. Anthropic puts it at about 75% cheaper than Haiku 4.5 on average. vendor It is the same list price as GPT-6 Luna.

Mistral Large 4 (Oct 6) is a natively multimodal mixture-of-experts model with about 1 trillion parameters, 52B of them active, priced at $1.36 / $4.18 per million tokens. vendor It is the top non-Chinese open-to-be model on Artificial Analysis, but still trails the best Chinese open models. independent

Reflection AI’s Beam (Oct 5) has 501B parameters, 23B of them active, with Apache 2.0 weights promised “later this month.” It claims 80.9 on SWE-bench Verified, a figure nobody has checked independently yet. vendor

Anthropic also launched monthly API credits for subscribers: $100 on Max 5×, $200 on Max 20× and up to $500 pooled for Team. vendor

06Chat apps go visual

GPT-6 reached every ChatGPT tier on Oct 7 and 8, with “Intelligent UI” answers that include charts, forms, maps and calculators. ChatGPT has more than 1.2 billion weekly users. press

A day later Anthropic launched Claude Dashboards, which turn plain-language questions into live dashboards over sources such as Snowflake and BigQuery, and Claude Motion, which makes short animated explainers. Motion uses code-based animation, not a video-generation model. Both are in beta. vendor

07Agents at scale, and decision models

Claude’s new “dynamic workflows” let a lead agent write a plan that runs across up to 1,000 agents in phases. Anthropic planted 70 bugs in a 116,000-line codebase: a single agent found 14–27 per run, while the workflow consistently found 66. This is Anthropic’s only published test of the feature, and Anthropic warns it can use “a lot of tokens.” vendor

OpenAI’s Decisions API went into public beta on Oct 6. It returns probabilities, choices and scores instead of text, and OpenAI says it is up to 10× faster than calling GPT-6 Luna through the Responses API. vendor Watch the driving-game demo on X. Microsoft followed on Oct 9 with Microsoft-Decision-1. vendor

08Money: a revenue wobble

On Oct 8 the FT reported that OpenAI had told investors its annualized revenue was about $50B at the end of September, against the roughly $68–70B reported a week earlier. Per a person familiar with the matter, the larger figure included gross revenue from partners. press

AI stocks fell that Thursday: CoreWeave about 8%, Oracle about 6% and Nvidia about 3%. press A day later, Bloomberg reported that OpenAI expects at least $70B by the end of 2026 and is in talks to raise $30B+ at a $1.4T pre-money valuation. press Separately, Bloomberg reports DeepSeek is close to raising at least $12B, with Tencent and CATL among the backers; DeepSeek hasn’t announced it. press

09Washington and the courts

  • Super Intelligence Force (Oct 4): led by Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton, with FTC Chair Andrew Ferguson among its members, and 120 days to report on risks and opportunities. press
  • FTC: a senior official told Semafor the agency is close to sending civil investigative demands to OpenAI and Anthropic, and possibly the evaluator METR. press
  • Pentagon: a Defense Department official told the BBC it “has ceased the use of Anthropic products,” though sources told the BBC Claude was in use until the week before. press
  • Grok Bot: Elon Musk says it will route some tasks to Claude Opus 5.5, Midjourney and Suno. vendor
  • USA TODAY Co. v. OpenAI (Oct 8): 19 publications allege their articles were used to train ChatGPT and seek more than $250M. These are allegations. press

11Handle with care

  • Every OpenAI math figure is OpenAI’s own. 722 was the launch-day count; the repository now lists 719.
  • The Buckmaster quote is secondhand, via @unusual_whales.
  • The attribution to OpenAI is Wikimedia’s belief, and OpenAI hasn’t confirmed the outage link.
  • OpenAI’s $50B, $70B and $1.4T figures come from anonymous sources in paywalled reports.
  • The FTC detail rests on a single official; the Pentagon story is relayed from the BBC.
  • Beam’s benchmarks, the planted-bugs test and the Decisions API speed claim are all vendor-run.
  • Despite claims online, no source confirms that xAI pays Anthropic when Grok Bot uses Claude.
