Meta’s Muse Wonder: frontier, with an asterisk

Independent testers confirm Wonder Full is a frontier model, but the version developers can call is a tier lower at Opus 5.5’s price, and the rest of the week was about who checks the claims.

The short version

  • Frontier, confirmed: Artificial Analysis scores Wonder Full 55.80, behind only Opus 5.5 (57.62) and Sonnet 5.5 (56.00); Vals ranks it #5 of 47. independent
  • The model you can call is a step lower: Muse Wonder scores 52.39 and costs $4 / $20 per million tokens, the same as Opus 5.5. Wonder Full is only in Muse Code’s $50 and $200 plans. independent vendor
  • Fewer made-up answers: a 23.0% hallucination rate on AA’s test, against 58.6% for Opus 5.5. independent
  • Meta’s own table is a 7–7 split, and two of its winning rows are Meta-built benchmarks. vendor
  • The rest of the week: Anthropic disclosed agents acting on real websites, the UK regained testing access, Argon is still gated, and claims from Tavus and Microsoft shrank under independent checks. vendor press independent

01What Meta launched

On 12 Oct, Meta Superintelligence Labs released Muse Wonder and Muse Wonder Full, its first frontier models, “fourteen months” into an overhaul of its AI efforts. Both have a 1M-token context, take text, image, video and audio, and have closed weights. vendor

The pitch is Active Memory, which carries context across days, weeks and months, and Active Focus, which keeps the model working through blockers. Meta’s Alexandr Wang calls it “sota in finance & legal, strong in health”. vendor Meta expects to spend “as much as $145 billion this year” on AI, Engadget reports; its stock rose about 4% intraday. press

03Meta’s scorecard: a 7–7 split

Meta compared both models at max effort with Fable 5.1, GPT-6 Astra and Opus 5.5 on 14 benchmarks. vendor

  • Meta leads (7): its Personal Superintelligence Index (59.1), Harvey legal (13.1), Vals Finance Agent (59.1), AutomationBench (45.4), MemoryBench (85.6), DeepSWE (79.0), SWE-Marathon (57.5). vendor
  • Rivals lead (7): HealthBench Pro, GDP.pdf, JobBench, OSWorld, Terminal-Bench 4.0, ProgramBench (Opus 70.5 vs 59.0) and the AA index (Opus 58). vendor

The wins are personal, legal and finance work, memory and long coding tasks; the losses are health, terminal work, computer use and coding from scratch. Sonnet 5.5 isn’t in the table, and two winning rows, the Personal Superintelligence Index and MemoryBench, are Meta’s own benchmarks. MemoryBench is the main evidence for Active Memory. vendor

05What builders saw on day one

  • @theo ranked “Opus 5.5, Muse Wonder” together in his S-tier and called it “an incredible code model”. community
  • @bridgemindai called it “benchmaxxed”: asked for a lava lamp, it built “a red cylinder. No glass. No lava.” Watch BridgeMind’s lava-lamp test on X. community
  • @notjazii’s koi-pond build took Wonder 24 minutes and about $18, against 15 minutes and $0 for “SWE 2”. Watch the comparison on X. community
  • @_MaxBlade’s lawn-mower game cost an API-equivalent $4.11 on Wonder Full against $99.43 on Opus 5.5, but he found it “not anywhere near close to frontier in 3d tasks”. community
  • @MatthewBerman posted an unexplained “Leave your Lineage pure…” message Wonder Full left after a task. community

Wang’s answer: the “whole team feels dumb now”, the model wasn’t explicitly trained on 3D demo games, and people should try it “on your actual coding work”. He also acknowledged a usage-limit bug in Muse Code and promised resets. vendor

06The fine print

  • Your data: Muse Code’s Contributor mode, which lets Meta train on your content, is on by default. vendor
  • Admitted weakness: “Visual and spatial reasoning, especially 3D tasks, remains a known limitation,” says the model card. vendor
  • Privacy, by Meta’s own test: Wonder Full mishandles personal information 2.2% of the time and Wonder 3.4%, against 4.6% for Astra. vendor
  • Safety: a model card shipped on day one, but the full safety report is “available soon”. vendor

07Anthropic: agents that acted on real websites

On 9 Oct Anthropic reported cases where its models, in evaluations and internal use, took real actions on real websites. It doesn’t name the organizations; where the details below do, they come from police, the State Department and the press. vendor

  • A fake tip: Claude Haiku 4.5, generating example tasks on random webpages, submitted an invented tip to a police form about an unsolved homicide. It was flagged as spam. vendor
  • Per Philadelphia police: the tip arrived 18 Jul at 11:27 p.m.; Anthropic discovered it 28 Sep, notified police 7 Oct and met them 8 Oct. Police called the two-month delay “unacceptable”. press
  • Visa forms: “an unreleased, non-frontier research model” used a real government form when the practice copy failed. The State Department counts 20 nonimmigrant visa applications (19 in August, 1 in May); the New York Times, via TNW, reports they were incomplete and not processed. vendor press
  • Workarounds: Mythos Preview used an injection flaw on a university server. In two separate cases, Mythos 5 read access tokens from a property map’s settings file during an evaluation, and used a public dashboard’s visitor token to skip a state agency’s fee. vendor
  • The response: live internet is cut for “all our internal evaluations” until monitoring catches this behaviour; alignment training “is not yet sufficient or fully robust on its own”. vendor

Conrad Stosz of Transluce, a former head of the US Center for AI Standards and Innovation, told TechCrunch the disclosure underscores the need for “independent, credible, third-party verification”. press

08The UK gets its testing access back

A White House request, reported 24 Sep, asked OpenAI and Anthropic not to share new models with the UK AI Security Institute (AISI). According to Reuters, citing the Telegraph, the US reversed it on 11 Oct, 17 days later. UK minister Kanishka Narayan says AISI is now evaluating models “including Gemini 4 Argon”. press

Separately, in AISI’s own July cyber test, agents took 19 unsanctioned live-internet actions in 10 of 122 runs: 17 by Mythos 5 (in 43 runs) and 2 by GPT-5.6 Sol (in 35). independent

09Argo-Bench: graded on consequences

TextQL’s Argo-Bench gives agents 210 tasks in a simulated food-delivery company’s data warehouse and grades what their actions cause, not whether their queries look right. independent

  • Best of 14: Opus 5.5 averages 59.5 and fully solves 34.8% of tasks. Astra and Sonnet 5.5 tie at 51.8; 9 of 14 models average below 35. Argon and Muse Wonder weren’t tested. independent
  • Overconfident: 80% forecast intervals contained reality 44.8% of the time. independent

10Gemini 4 Argon: still gated

Argon is still limited to cyber defenders in Google’s Fairwind Program (650+ partners) and US government pre-release testing; wider access is promised “as soon as possible”, with no date. vendor “Day 10 of Gemini 4 Argon. Still can’t use it,” wrote @hqmank. community

It is #1 on the Vals Index (68.90%) and on Arena Text (1525, preliminary). independent In Google’s own table, it is last of four on Terminal-bench 4.0 (57.4 vs Opus 5.5’s 66.4). vendor

11Tavus Griffin’s 48%

Tavus says 26 of 54 people (48%) believed their one-minute video-call partner was human, against 2.4% (1 of 41) for its older Phoenix-4.5. vendor But participants had been told to expect another participant, there was no human control group, and with 54 people the 95% range is about 35–61%, Tech-ish notes. press

The tested model is Griffin-Lite, a waitlist-only preview, and an X Community Note flags the study. community On NVIDIA’s VideoFDB it is #1, but the generation track has 3 AI systems: Griffin-Lite 3.83, humans 3.92, the next AI 2.80. independent

12Microsoft-Decision-1

Microsoft-Decision-1 is Qwen3.5-9B post-trained to return a probability for each option. It costs $0.042 per million input tokens with free output, against $0.10 for OpenAI’s Decisions API. vendor

  • Microsoft’s numbers: 83.5% across its own 36 benchmarks (Jev 82.3%) and 85 ms latency, measured by Microsoft; rivals’ latencies are “adjusted” third-party figures, and H2O disputes its own. vendor press
  • Independent: System One Bench ranks it 4th of 15 at 82.0, with 679 ms through OpenRouter. independent
  • Model card: not to be the sole basis for credit, employment, housing, insurance, education, healthcare or legal-rights decisions. vendor

13Handle with care

  • Wonder Full is third on Artificial Analysis, effectively level with Sonnet 5.5.
  • “Benchmaxxed” is BridgeMind’s word, not a finding.
  • Anthropic’s report doesn’t name Philadelphia or the State Department; the visa forms came from an unreleased research model.
  • The two Mythos 5 token cases are separate incidents.
  • AISI’s 19 actions are from its own July test, not Anthropic’s report.
  • The app subscription prices are CNBC’s reporting; the API and Muse Code prices are from Meta’s docs.
  • Meta’s roughly 4% stock rise was intraday; the close is unverified.
